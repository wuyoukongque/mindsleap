A customer wants to understand your product but does not visit your website first. They stay in an AI conversation, asking whether it suits them, how it compares with another product, and where to learn more. This is an imagined buying scenario, but it raises a practical question: if your website does not deliver the first introduction, what information are you prepared to let represent your business?

On November 10, OpenAI announced Businesses in ChatGPT in beta. Business Hub lets eligible businesses claim and maintain their information. Business Agents initially serve eligible ecommerce and online service businesses in the United States. Their eligibility scopes differ; this does not mean every business can already deploy a customer-facing agent.

What matters is not simply another account to manage. The way customers learn about a business may move further from browsing pages toward asking ongoing questions.

## From an Introduction Page to a Conversation with Follow-Up Questions

OpenAI describes an entry point where users can view the profile of a business mentioned in an answer and, if available, choose to start a conversation with its Business Agent. The announcement emphasizes that these conversations are clearly labeled and distinct from ChatGPT's independent answers.

That distinction matters. A business describing its own products and an independent system comparing alternatives have different responsibilities. Placing them in nearby interfaces should not leave customers confused about who is speaking.

For me, the more interesting issue is the depth of communication. A page can display products, but customer questions rarely follow its structure. Why does a specification matter? What happens if a condition is not met? Could a particular need make the service unsuitable altogether? Answering depends on whether a business has organized its actual operating knowledge, not merely whether it has an attractive introduction.

## What Businesses Need to Provide Is Not a Longer Brochure

According to the announcement, verified businesses can update their details and provide feeds containing prices and availability. To me, the practical implication is that marketing information needs an ongoing maintenance process. Being accurate on publication day is not enough.

Imagine an equipment business replacing a product model. The website is updated, but the sales deck still uses old specifications and distributors retain an outdated price list. If AI reads all those materials, it may not know which one represents the business's current commitments. This is a hypothetical information conflict, not a customer case from the announcement.

Salespeople have traditionally used experience to identify such differences and consulted product or finance colleagues when necessary. A conversational interface needs comparable rules: which information is authoritative, who updates it, and who resolves conflicts. More material does not necessarily mean better information, let alone promises that can be fulfilled.

**A business's ability to express itself through AI still depends on its own understanding of what it does.**

## A Sales Interface Willing to Say “Not Suitable”

OpenAI's Business Agents policy requires businesses to represent their identity and information honestly. Agents must not invent prices, guarantees, or qualifications, or pretend to be independent advisers. One policy distinction deserves attention:

> Business verification alone does not guarantee agent eligibility.

From an operating perspective, proving that a company exists and allowing it to conduct a particular AI interaction are different matters. Businesses should likewise distinguish questions answerable from public information, questions requiring a professional, and questions outside the service's scope.

An unconstrained digital salesperson could turn every need into a reason to buy. It might appear enthusiastic in the short term, but mis-selling, delivery disputes, and after-sales costs eventually return to the business. A useful AI interface must retain the ability to say that something is unsuitable or needs further confirmation.

The same policy also prohibits continued sales pressure or follow-up after someone declines. That is more than a platform requirement; it is a basic principle of customer relationships. Becoming better at conversation should not mean becoming better at pestering people.

## Being Seen and Earning Trust Are Different Accounts

The announcement says Business Hub provides aggregated insights, such as mentions and website clicks, without giving businesses users' raw conversations. I see such data as useful for observing an entry point, not as a substitute for evaluating actual business results.

Even when mentions increase, businesses should ask whether customers understand them more accurately, inquiries are better matched, and staff spend less time repeatedly explaining the same things. More exposure accompanied by more misunderstanding may not create value.

I prefer to see GEO, or making business information easier for generative AI to understand and cite accurately, as information governance rather than another round of keyword stuffing. Clear service definitions, sourced facts, verifiable cases, and consistent product information are more valuable long-term assets than large volumes of contradictory copy. This is a business judgment, not a platform-promised ranking formula.

## Explain Yourself Clearly Before a New Entry Point Amplifies You

The product still has eligibility, geographic, and category restrictions. It does not establish that all businesses in China can use it directly, nor does claiming a profile guarantee recommendations. Current access conditions should be checked against official rules.

But businesses need not wait for a platform to open more broadly before organizing their information. What does the product solve? Where is it suitable or unsuitable? Which prices can be public? Which commitments require confirmation? These questions should already have clear internal answers.

For me, the most important question is not whether to rush into a new channel. It is more fundamental: **if AI answered a customer's first questions about your business tomorrow, which set of company information would you want it to rely on?**

Answering that gives a business a chance to turn new forms of reach into trustworthy customer relationships.
